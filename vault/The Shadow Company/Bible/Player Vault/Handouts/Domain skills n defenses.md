# Skills

During intrigue, each skill can be used to improve a domain’s defense levels or to target the opposed domain and reduce its defense levels. But it’s up to the players and the GM to imagine how this plays out, by translating skill tests and their effects into game terms.
## **Diplomacy:**
*Insight, Persuasion.*

Represent's a domains ability to get what it wants without resorting to war or espionage. Especially Alliances, which is not a mechanical thing, but a narrative of an agreement between two domains. A domain could for instance petition another domain for military aid, which will be in the form of lending a special unit, which each domain has. Only one unit can be provided, so get it before the enemy! Special units on loan return to their owners.

A hostile realm is not the same as a villain realm. A villain realm is actively trying to destroy the pc's. A hostile realm, merely dislike, distrust etc.. the attitudes are: hostile, suspicious, neutral, friendly, allied. 

The main thing an npc realm can aid with according to these rules specifically is the special unit, sitting out a battle (not aiding your enemy). But that does not mean you cannot come up with other forms of aid, retainers, powerful lieutenant or information and so forth. 

## **Espionage:**
*Investigation, Stealth*

Try to learn the secrets of other domains, 
- their plans, 
- their current activites, 
- who they are allied with, 
- learn the makeup of army (which units), 
- which allies will join the battle, 
- determine if they have magical resources or secrets for your Lore skill to research. 
- Usually Espionage targets one of the defenses of a domain (communications, resolve, resources). 

some examples of information to choose to learn:
- The target domain’s current defense scores
- The target domain’s current defense levels
- The target domain’s skill bonuses
- The target domain’s current units
- Another statistic or piece of information, at the GM’s discretion
For instance, knowing what kind of units the opposing organization has mustered can help plan your own mustering or if they are constantly boosting a specific defense maybe it's time to try and sabotage that.

## **Lore:**
*Arcana, History, Religion*

Lore represents  a domain's ability to research both magical and historical knowledge. It is a broad and wide ranging skill, which outside of Intrigue can be used to unlock arcane discoveries, dig up details of forgotten history, discover the existence of lost spells or reveal answers to ancient mysteries.. perhaps.

While Lore is almost limitless, the GM rules what is reasonable and the skill should never be used as a substitute for adventuring. 

A team of npc agents might be sent out to recover the headpiece of the staff of Ra as the PC's quest to find the map room in the hidden well of souls. 

Lore operations is not neccesarily just thumbing through 80 pages in a book. It can include active invistigative operations with agents in the field or getting knowledge in shady ways, perhaps even mystically dangerous ways.

Lore can also be used to learn magical secrets of an opposed domain. Such as supernatural attacks or defenses, or any famous magical items and extraordinary units in their army. 
## **Operations:**
*Associated Character Skills: Athletics, Insight *

Operations represents a domain’s ability to:
- muster new units, as a general rule this doesnt require a test unless GM decides that it can fail.
	- a domain officer can use a domain action during intrigue to muster a new unit under their command. 
	- an officer can control a number of units equal to their proficiency bonus.
	- Units mustered through alliances and diplomacy are controlled by a character but not owned by that character, they return to their npc realm after the battle.
- a domain officer can use a domain action during intrigue to spend gold on Upgrading/Modifying unit equipment: light to medium, medium to heavy, heavy to super heavy. The officer can upgrade as many units as he controls. Upgrading can go from light to superheavy in one upgrade. 
	- alliance units and levies cannot be upgraded
	- upgraded units retain their original tier
- perform many of the basic functions of maintaining a domain. 
- construct fortifications for battlefields, e.g. stone fence, tower, palisade and other fortifications
- This is intended to be a catchall domain skill for any activity the players or GM might think of that doesn’t easily fit into another skill.


**Disbanding Units**

Eventually, a domain will max out the number of units it controls, making it impossible to muster any new units. When this happens, a domain can disband any number of units to free up space
in its army. This isn’t an action, but is simply an order an officer can issue under the umbrella of the Operations skill. The domain does not regain any money spent on equipment upgrades for units that are disbanded.

**Beyond Intrigue**

The Operations domain skill covers many mundane activities outside of intrigue, including building roads or watchtowers, sending out a unit of scouts to explore the local wilderness, and so forth. Anything a domain might reasonably be able to do but which isn’t covered by another domain skill can be accomplished using Operations.

Not only do domain skills allow a domain to do things while the characters are adventuring, they allow the domain to do things outside the scope of adventuring, from research to sabotage to negotiation.
Players are encouraged to be creative with their domain’s skills, the same way players can improvise how they use a character’s abilities and skills. Don’t worry about “doing it right.”
But it’s up to the players and the GM to imagine how this plays out, by translating skill tests and their effects into game terms

You use a domain skill to improve a domain defense.
You use a domain skill to sabotage an opponent domain's defense.
You use a domain skill to put your Organization to other uses outside of intrigue. 

## Lowering a Domain’s Defenses

In the same manner, the players can target a villainous realm in the hopes of lowering that domain’s defense levels. An acting officer picks a skill and one of the opposed domain’s defenses, then describes how they intend to use that skill to compromise the defense. If the scenario makes sense to the GM, the officer rolls a d20, adds the domain skill’s modifier, adds their proficiency bonus if applicable, and compares the result with the enemy realm’s defense score. If the result is equal to or greater than the chosen defense’s score, that defense is lowered one level. If the player rolls a 20 on the check, the chosen defense is lowered two levels. The level of any defense cannot be decreased below -3.

Villainous realms can target the defenses of the heroes’ organization in the same way, with the GM making the decision as to which officer, skill, and specific approach are involved.

*For example, Anna (playing a character named Judge) wants to raise the Communications level of her group’s mercenary company, the Chain of Acheron, using that organization’s Espionage domain skill. She tells the GM: “I want to require all our agents to use only verbal communications, nothing written down, and to use our secret battle language.”

*This is a great idea—but it’s not necessarily easy to implement. The Chain of Acheron has a lot of soldiers, agents, and allies in the city, so there’s no guarantee that these orders will work well enough to make a difference. To make the domain skill test, Anna rolls a d20 and adds the Espionage bonus for Acheron’s Chain. She also adds Judge’s proficiency bonus, since Judge is proficient in Investigation and has not already used their bonus during this intrigue (you may only use proficiency bonus once pr character the entire Intrigue).*
# Defenses

## Communications
This defense is a measure of how rapidly and accurately informations are transmitted between officers and the npc's of the organization. 

Communications directly affects an army's ability to coordinate its activities. 
A high communications (must be bolstered from 0 each intrigue) allows an army to manuever into a better starting deployment. 
Poor or compromised communications lets a domain's enemies know what its officers and agents are doing and can interfere with deployment by false signals & missives. 
This takes the form of repositioning your units on the battlefield, either by your domain or the opposed domain to units being removed from battle. 
## Resolve
This defense measures the commitment the domain's npc's has to its cause. It depends on many factors, including how well the followers understand the domain's cause. The domain by default shares the philosophy of its officers and leaders. 
Resolve directly affects the officers. If everyone working for the domain is committed and engaged, it makes training easier and inspires hope and confidence in victory. The reverse is also true. 
High resolve for a battle means the officers are energized, coordinated and convinced of victory. This grants them benefits in combat against the opposed domain's officers. The reverse is also true. 
This takes the form of advantages/disadvantages, speed increase/decrease.

## Resources
This is a measure of the domain's wealth. Though this defense represents more than money. The resources includes whatever a domain values and collects. Gold, knowledge, secrets or more esoteric things. 
A high Resource affects the gear of the domain's units, granting them improved power or damage. Poor resource affects morale, resembling troops not being fed or armor and weapons in need of repairs.
This takes the form of extra damage, adv. on power tests to disadv. on morale and command test.
