# Taxation System of the Kingdom of Ursia

## Overview
The Kingdom of Ursia operates under a traditional feudal taxation system where land ownership and resource extraction form the primary basis for tax obligations. Unlike modern systems, individual income and personal wealth are generally not subject to taxation. The system is administered through a hierarchy from the Crown down to local Barons.

## Primary Tax Categories

### 1. Royal Land Tribute (The King's Due)
**Purpose**: Fund the Crown, Royal Guards, and central government
**Rate**: 10-15% of assessed land value annually
**Collection**: Local Barons collect and forward to Arendur
**Assessment**: Based on acreage, land quality, and granted privileges
**Records**: Maintained by House Volantis (Royal Treasury administrators)

**Example**: A 500-acre lumber grant might be assessed at 500 gold annual value, requiring 50-75 gold in Royal Tribute.

### 2. Baronial Land Tax (The Lord's Due)
**Purpose**: Fund local administration, infrastructure, and defense
**Rate**: 5-10% of assessed land value annually
**Collection**: Baron's appointed tax collectors
**Flexibility**: Can vary based on local needs and Baron's discretion
**Typical Combined Rate**: 15-25% total (Royal + Baronial)

### 3. Resource Tithes (Production Tax)
**Purpose**: Compensation for use of Crown/Baronial resources
**Method**: Percentage of raw materials extracted
**Common Rates**:
- Timber: 1 in every 10 logs
- Mining: 1 in every 8 loads of ore
- Quarrying: 1 in every 12 stones
- Fishing: 1 in every 15 catches (rivers/lakes only)

**Collection**: Physical delivery to Baron's warehouses or designated collection points

### 4. Trade Tariffs and Market Fees
**Purpose**: Regulate commerce and fund trade infrastructure
**Types**:
- **Border Tariffs**: Goods entering/leaving barony (2-5% of cargo value)
- **Market Fees**: Stall rental and sales permissions (flat daily rates)
- **Export Licenses**: Permission to trade outside the kingdom (annual fees)
- **Guild Fees**: Professional licensing for crafters and merchants

## What Is NOT Taxed

### Individual Commoners
- **No Income Tax**: Workers' wages are not subject to taxation
- **No Personal Property Tax**: Tools, livestock, household goods exempt
- **Alternative Obligations**: Military service, labor corvée, or rent payments to landowners

### Personal Wealth
- **Noble Assets**: Personal treasure, jewelry, weapons not taxed
- **Inheritance**: Wealth transfers freely within families
- **Exception**: Land grants may require Crown approval for inheritance

### Internal Trade
- **Local Commerce**: Sales within a barony typically exempt from additional taxes
- **Barter Systems**: Non-monetary exchanges generally not taxed
- **Personal Use**: Goods produced and consumed by the same household

## Emergency and Special Taxation

### Wartime Levies
**Legal Basis**: Barons may impose temporary additional taxes during genuine emergencies
**Limitations**:
- Must be genuinely temporary (typically one year maximum)
- Cannot exceed 50% increase over standard rates
- Requires formal justification to the Privy Council
- Subject to Crown review and potential reversal

**Common Triggers**: Foreign invasion, natural disasters, major infrastructure needs

### Crown Assessments
**Special Royal Demands**: The King may request additional tribute for specific purposes
**Examples**: Royal weddings, major wars, new construction projects
**Process**: Formal decree through the Privy Council to all Barons

## Administration and Enforcement

### The Collection Hierarchy
1. **Crown Level**: House Volantis oversees royal tribute collection
2. **Ducal Level**: Major Houses coordinate regional collection
3. **Baronial Level**: Local administrators manage day-to-day collection
4. **Village Level**: Town speakers and local officials assist with assessment

### Tax Collector Roles and Powers
**Assessment Authority**: Can inspect lands, buildings, and production facilities
**Record Keeping**: Must maintain detailed records of all collections
**Enforcement Powers**: Can seize property for non-payment (with Baron's approval)
**Limitations**: Cannot enter noble residences without permission

### Common Assessment Methods
**Land Quality**: Fertile farmland taxed higher than rocky or swampy areas
**Resource Access**: Proximity to rivers, forests, or mines affects assessment
**Infrastructure**: Good roads and market access increase taxable value
**Historical Production**: Past yields used to estimate future obligations

## Regional Variations

### Arendur (Urban)
- **Higher Rates**: 20-30% total due to city services and infrastructure
- **Complex System**: Multiple overlapping jurisdictions and guild requirements
- **Professional Administration**: Full-time bureaucrats with detailed records
- **Trade Focus**: Heavy emphasis on tariffs and commercial licensing

### Rural Baronies (Bedegaarn, Dalrath, etc.)
- **Simpler Methods**: Primarily land and resource-based
- **Personal Relationships**: Collectors often know landowners personally
- **Seasonal Collection**: Timed around harvests and major production cycles
- **Greater Flexibility**: More room for negotiation and payment plans

### Border Regions
- **Military Considerations**: Reduced rates in exchange for border defense obligations
- **Trade Routes**: Heavy tariff income from merchant traffic
- **Special Arrangements**: Unique deals based on strategic importance

## Common Tax Avoidance and Disputes

### Legitimate Strategies
**Productivity Claims**: Arguing for lower assessments due to poor yields or conditions
**Infrastructure Investment**: Major improvements can temporarily reduce obligations
**Military Service**: Personal or family military service may reduce tax burden
**Religious Donation**: Large gifts to temples may earn tax considerations

### Suspicious Activities (From Tax Collector Perspective)
**Resource Diversion**: Hiding production before tithes are calculated
**False Partnerships**: Creating shell arrangements to confuse ownership
**Smuggling**: Avoiding tariffs through illegal export routes
**Assessment Fraud**: Misrepresenting land quality or productive capacity

### Common Disputes
**Land Boundaries**: Disagreements over what land is subject to taxation
**Quality Assessment**: Arguments over productive value of holdings
**Emergency Duration**: Challenges to the continuation of wartime levies
**Collection Methods**: Disputes over when and how taxes should be paid

## Current Political Issues (648 Z.E.)

### House Volantis Crisis
- Duke Bartolo Volantis missing, affecting royal tribute collection
- Duchess Elvira refusing to perform duties until husband returns
- King selling Privy Council seats to guilds for immediate revenue
- Old noble families angered by "new money" gaining political power

### Baron Hexton's Irregularities
- Imposing excessive "emergency" levies beyond legal authority
- Selective enforcement favoring political allies
- Using emergency funds for personal political negotiations
- Attempting non-traditional taxation methods (inventory, profit-based)

### Guild Political Pressure
- Ruby Throne and other major guilds challenging traditional feudal taxation
- Demands for representation proportional to economic contribution
- Conflicts between guild commercial interests and noble land rights
- Questions about taxation of purely commercial (non-land-based) enterprises

## Practical Examples

### Ranch Falca Situation
**Standard Obligations**:
- Land Tax: 60 gold annually (500 acres at moderate assessment)
- Timber Tithe: 1 log per 10 cut (delivered to Baron's stockpile)
- Export Tariff: 3% of lumber sold outside Bedegaarn Barony

**Current Problem**: Production increased 50% but tithe deliveries remain static, suggesting either hoarding, smuggling, or some arrangement diverting lumber before official counting.

### The Milly Bell Shop
**Standard Obligations**:
- Business License: 5 gold annually (paid to town)
- Market Stall Fee: 1 silver per market day
- Import Tariff: 2% on goods brought from other baronies

**Why Tax Documents Matter**: As a legitimate business, Millicent would naturally accumulate official correspondence about tax matters, making her shop a logical place for collectors to conduct business.
