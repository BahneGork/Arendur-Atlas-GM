# Tax Enforcement in the Kingdom of Ursia
*Game Master Reference Guide*

## Legal Enforcement Powers

### Property-Based Enforcement
- **Land Confiscation**: Baron can revoke land grants for persistent non-payment
- **Asset Seizure**: Tools, livestock, stored goods taken until debts paid
- **Warehouse Raids**: Tax collectors with guard escort seize untithed resources
- **Business Closure**: Revoking licenses and sealing premises until compliance

### Personal Enforcement
- **Debtor's Prison**: Short-term detention until family/associates pay debts
- **Labor Corvée**: Working off debt through forced labor on public projects
- **Military Impressment**: Conscription as alternative to cash payment
- **Public Stocks**: Humiliation punishment for tax evasion

## Escalating Enforcement Procedures

### Stage 1: Administrative Pressure
**Typical Timeline**: 30-60 days after due date
- Official written demands with increasing urgency
- "Penalty" assessments that raise future tax obligations
- Temporary license suspension for businesses
- Blocking access to guild financing or trade partnerships

**DC 12 Persuasion/Intimidation**: Collector convinces cooperation
**Failure**: Escalates to Stage 2

### Stage 2: Social Pressure
**Typical Timeline**: 60-90 days after due date
- Names posted at market squares and temples (public shaming)
- Guild exclusion and professional organization refusal
- Trade embargos from other businesses
- Religious sanctions (priests refuse services)

**DC 15 Persuasion/Deception**: Taxpayer negotiates alternative arrangement
**Failure**: Escalates to Stage 3

### Stage 3: Physical Enforcement
**Typical Timeline**: 90+ days after due date
- Armed guards accompany collectors
- Tax officials occupy defaulter's property
- Mandatory inventory of all assets and production
- Travel restrictions (cannot leave barony)

**DC 18 Intimidation/Investigation**: Enforcement team locates hidden assets
**Success**: Recovery of 50-75% of owed taxes
**Failure**: Escalates to Stage 4

### Stage 4: Severe Sanctions
**Typical Timeline**: 6+ months of resistance
- Land grant revocation and return to Baron's control
- Family and business partner liability extension
- Criminal prosecution for theft from the Crown
- Exile/outlawry (rare, reserved for major cases)

**No skill checks**: Legal process with predetermined outcomes

## Regional Enforcement Variations

### Urban Arendur Methods
- **Professional Bureaucracy**: Full-time enforcement officers, detailed records
- **Guild Cooperation**: Professional organizations assist member compliance
- **Formal Court System**: Legal proceedings for disputes and enforcement
- **City Watch Integration**: Guards routinely assist enforcement actions

*Higher success rates but more legal constraints*

### Rural Baronial Methods
- **Personal Relationships**: Collectors know families, use social pressure
- **Seasonal Timing**: Enforcement around harvests when resources available
- **Barter Arrangements**: Accepting goods/services instead of coin
- **Community Pressure**: Village leaders encouraged to enforce compliance

*Lower success rates but more flexible approaches*

## Creative Enforcement Methods

### Economic Warfare
- **Supply Chain Disruption**: Block access to necessary trade goods
- **Financial Isolation**: Prevent banking and credit access
- **Market Exclusion**: Ban sales at official markets and fairs
- **Resource Control**: Limit access to mills, forges, transportation

### Information Warfare
- **Surveillance Networks**: Monitor production and trade activities
- **Incentivized Reporting**: Reward informants revealing tax evasion
- **Documentation Requirements**: Demand extensive compliance paperwork
- **Audit Threats**: Random "inspections" disrupting business

---

## Taxpayer Resistance Methods

### Legal Resistance
- **Hidden Production**: Conceal actual output before assessment
- **Legal Appeals**: Challenge through proper channels to higher authority
- **Collective Action**: Multiple taxpayers coordinate resistance
- **Documentation Games**: Maintain multiple sets of books

### Criminal Evasion
- **Smuggling Operations**: Move goods through unofficial channels
- **Bribery Networks**: Corrupt collectors to reduce assessments
- **False Partnerships**: Create shell arrangements to confuse ownership
- **Physical Violence**: Armed resistance against collection attempts

**Investigation DCs**:
- Hidden Production: DC 15 Investigation
- False Records: DC 18 Investigation (Insight)
- Smuggling Routes: DC 16 Survival (tracking)
- Bribery Networks: DC 20 Investigation (social)

---

## Enforcement Complications

### Political Constraints
- **Noble Immunity**: Cannot easily enforce against powerful houses
- **Jurisdictional Conflicts**: Overlapping authority between officials
- **Appeal Rights**: Legal processes that delay enforcement
- **Protected Interests**: Some taxpayers have higher authority backing

### Practical Limitations
- **Resource Costs**: Enforcement often costs more than recovered taxes
- **Geographic Challenges**: Remote properties difficult to monitor
- **Seasonal Timing**: Collection limited by agricultural cycles
- **Economic Disruption**: Harsh enforcement reduces future tax base

## Adventure Hooks & Scenarios

### Direct Involvement
- PCs hired as enforcement muscle for dangerous collections
- PCs witness tax collector murder, must investigate
- PCs' allies targeted by questionable emergency assessments
- PCs discover evidence of collector corruption

### Indirect Consequences
- Tax rebellion disrupts trade routes PCs use
- Harsh enforcement creates refugees seeking PC help
- Collector discovers PC illegal activities during routine audit
- Political resistance creates opportunities for PC faction advancement

### Investigation Opportunities
- Missing tax collector with evidence of Baron's illegal methods
- Mysterious increase in ranch production (supernatural aid?)
- Smuggling network larger than expected (ties to major conspiracy?)
- Tax records reveal patterns pointing to greater mysteries
